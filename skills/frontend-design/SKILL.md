# Frontend Design

Create a usable frontend with a deliberate aesthetic that fits its subject. Work within the existing stack and product behavior, and inspect the rendered result for substantial UI changes.

## Before coding

Understand the request, audience, product domain, and existing interface. Inspect nearby code, shared components, assets, and project conventions. Choose a concrete visual concept that gives the work a recognizable point of view. The direction may be restrained or expressive; consistency and fit matter more than intensity.

Decide the information hierarchy, content, page structure, and key interaction states before polishing. Preserve product behavior and established patterns unless the user asks for a redesign. Use truthful, specific copy and domain-relevant visuals. Do not fabricate metrics, endorsements, customers, or capabilities.

## Match the interface to its job

For a landing page, make the actual product, person, venue, or offer clear in the opening viewport. Use a literal subject heading and support it with concrete value language. Choose a hero composition based on the domain rather than defaulting to a familiar split layout. Let the page structure preview what follows and avoid generic sections without meaningful content.

For an application, dashboard, editor, or operational tool, optimize repeated-use ergonomics, scanning, navigation, and information density. For games, portfolios, and creative or consumer experiences, allow more atmosphere and custom interaction when it helps the task.

## Make visual choices intentional

Choose typography, color, spacing, imagery, and motion as a coherent system. Avoid relying on interchangeable defaults—such as generic gradient decoration, repetitive card grids, vague SaaS copy, or decorative UI chrome—unless the product gives them a clear reason. Use familiar controls for actions, and give every interactive element an understandable role and state.

Keep responsive behavior stable across narrow viewports, text scaling, and long content. Build semantic markup, keyboard access, visible focus, contrast, and reduced-motion support into the interface. Respect the project's performance and dependency conventions.

## Verify

When the app can run locally, inspect a desktop and mobile rendering in a browser after substantial changes. Fix blank renders, overlap, clipping, broken imagery, weak contrast, broken navigation, and console errors before finishing. Check the relevant focus, active, loading, empty, and error states for controls you changed. Calibrate the amount of verification to the size and risk of the change, and report what you actually checked.
